# Public cases and disclosed methods — working research corpus

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-15. Summarized for original educational / research-method use. Each item distinguishes publicly disclosed activity from the **transferable analytic question**. This is not a proprietary playbook or endorsement. Sources can change; verify dates and continued availability when external research is permitted. Claims about public cases do not establish facts about Aquaverse.

## Goldman Sachs — banking, structured finance and investment underwriting

**GS-01 — Bedford Stuyvesant Restoration Corporation, Brooklyn, mixed-use/mixed-income finance.**
The firm describes financing six mixed-use mixed-income housing developments in one Brooklyn neighborhood, alongside a separate lending facility to the community development organization.
- Source: Goldman Sachs, [Sustainable Finance — Inclusive Growth, Bedford Stuyvesant case](https://www.goldmansachs.com/our-firm/sustainable-finance/inclusive-growth).
- Transferable method (our inference): break a multi-asset destination into financeable uses; analyze cash-flow stability, borrower/operator, community benefit, collateral, liquidity, and risk of one asset subsidizing another. A disclosed partnership is **not** evidence that comparable finance will be available in Thailand.

**GS-02 — Public structured and real estate financing capabilities.**
The published financing practice describes project-finance-backed structures, commercial real estate debt, loan syndication/securitization, and sustainable banking.
- Source: Goldman Sachs, [Investment Banking — Financing](https://www.goldmansachs.com/what-we-do/investment-banking/financing/).
- Transferable method: build separate funding and repayment cases for hotel, residential, retail, MICE, park, infrastructure; model recourse, covenants, security, base/downside DSCR and sources/uses **before** consolidating.

**GS-03 — Goldman Sachs Asset Management infrastructure underwriting.**
The infrastructure strategy publicly emphasizes energy transition, digital infrastructure, transport/logistics and circular economy, plus disciplined underwriting and risk management.
- Source: Goldman Sachs AM, [Infrastructure](https://am.gs.com/en-us/advisors/products/infrastructure).
- Transferable method: insist on an asset-specific investment thesis, downside, explicit diligence gates, controllable risks and operating capability. The strategy and screening statistics disclosed by GS are **not** financing eligibility criteria for this project.

**GS-04 — Sustainable finance and project funding examples.**
The firm's public sustainable finance pages describe climate transition finance and investment activity (including named renewable industrial development cases).
- Source: Goldman Sachs, [Climate Transition](https://www.goldmansachs.com/our-firm/sustainable-finance/climate-transition).
- Transferable method: separate capital to create an asset from working capital, transition risk and cash-flow funding; verify whether green finance eligibility is real and locally available. Never transfer an issuer's announced target or a case's initial financing narrative into Aquaverse assumptions.

## McKinsey — capital projects, tech deployment and destination readiness

**MK-01 — Project delivery hub for a delayed multibillion-dollar industrial build.**
McKinsey documents use of project analytics, productivity and work-package variance to reveal schedule recovery opportunities.
- Source: McKinsey (2021), [Project delivery hubs: Analytics for better megaproject outcomes](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/project-delivery-hubs-analytics-for-better-megaproject-outcomes).
- Transferable method: verify work-breakdown structure; compare physical progress against baseline; track critical-path dependencies, resource/permit readiness, decision owners, and leading indicators—not just status colors. Industrial-sector outcomes are not transferable cost-saving percentages.

**MK-02 — Capital Excellence project development/delivery.**
Its published practice describes front-end assessment, value levers in design, procurement, execution rhythm, capability and risk management.
- Source: McKinsey, [Capital Excellence](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/how-we-help-clients/capital-excellence).
- Transferable method: stage-gate project scope; formal option appraisal; distinguish concept, designed, permitted, procured, commissioned, operating; require schedule and constructability evidence.

**MK-03 — AI and digital connected across the capital-project life cycle.**
A 2026 McKinsey article describes design, procurement, commissioning, cost/schedule and portfolio management; includes disclosed examples of project delivery dashboards, option analysis and schedule optimization.
- Source: McKinsey (2026), [Why the future of capital projects belongs to digital and AI-enabled teams](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/why-the-future-of-capital-projects-belongs-to-digital-and-ai-enabled-teams).
- Transferable method: assess technology as a decision-system workflow, not a tool list; baseline time/cost, data availability, responsible operator, integration and value realized. Never import case-study savings rates into the V4.0-only model.

**MK-04 — Tourist-flow management and destination carrying capacity.**
McKinsey's tourism analysis evaluates destination stress across local community, visitor experience, infrastructure, environment, and heritage; outlines visitor redistribution and financing considerations.
- Source: McKinsey (2024), [Destination readiness](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/travel/our-insights/destination-readiness-preparing-for-the-tourist-flows-of-tomorrow).
- Transferable method: map peak-day/season demand to roads, parking, water, wastewater, staff, emergency response, hotel rooms and attraction capacity. A geographic sample from the article is not a demand forecast for Chonburi.

## Morgan Stanley — syndication, bankability and risk allocation

**MS-01 — Battery Park City resiliency and BART green/sustainability bonds.**
Morgan Stanley describes underwriting sustainability-labeled municipal financings for flood adaptation, transit and related public assets.
- Source: Morgan Stanley, [Sustainability bonds focus on a more livable future](https://www.morganstanley.com/ideas/sustainability-bonds-more-livable-future).
- Transferable method: identify eligible use of proceeds, repayable cash flows, issuers, investors, covenants, verification and disclosure. US municipal tax exemption and structures are jurisdiction-specific and **must not** be assumed available in Thailand.

**MS-02 — Rattlesnake and Briscoe wind project financing.**
The firm describes nonrecourse construction/term debt and tax-equity partnership structures for named US wind projects.
- Source: Morgan Stanley, [Green Bond Program — wind portfolio examples](https://www.morganstanley.com/articles/green-bond-program).
- Transferable method: separate construction draw/risks from stabilized cash flows; link debt sizing to contracted revenue and acceptable risk; identify what would need to exist for nonrecourse finance. US tax equity is not a Thailand financing assumption.

**MS-03 — Infrastructure Partners portfolio and investment approach.**
Morgan Stanley publishes holdings in water/waste, energy, digital and transport (e.g., Valoriza, Seven Seas Water, Flexential) and describes active ownership and governance.
- Sources: Morgan Stanley, [Infrastructure investment approach](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-us/institutional-investor/about-us/people-and-teams/investment-teams/infrastructure-team.html) and [Infrastructure portfolio](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-us/capital-seeker/infrastructure/portfolio/infrastructure.html).
- Transferable method: map investable SPVs, cash-flow predictability, management controls, operating governance, holding period, exit and operational improvement plans. Portfolio holdings alone provide no indication of investor interest in Aquaverse.

## JLL — hospitality, mixed-use and destination feasibility

**JLL-01 — Plaza Coral Gables multi-asset management.**
JLL discusses the Plaza Coral Gables as a mixed-use property combining office, retail, residential and shared amenities and its need for integrated property/experience management.
- Source: JLL (2024), [Why the stakes are high in mixed-use property management](https://www.jll.com/en-uk/guides/why-the-stakes-are-high-in-mixed-use-property-management).
- Transferable method: validate cross-asset footfall dependencies, different operating hours and customer segments, shared infrastructure cost allocation, tenant activation, and whole-destination experience. Do not treat this as a local demand comparable.

**JLL-02 — The Corbin District, phased mixed-use financing.**
JLL reports arranging financing for phase II of a mixed-use development in Darien, with separate construction lending and C-PACE sources, and discusses pre-leasing.
- Source: JLL (2024), [JLL arranges $165M construction financing for The Corbin District](https://www.jll.com/en-us/newsroom/jll-arranges-165m-construction-financing-for-the-corbin-district).
- Transferable method: test individual phase bankability, actual pre-leasing, anchor-tenant risk, and cost of finance; US C-PACE is **not** presumed available in Thailand.

**JLL-03 — Hotel asset management aligned with development delivery.**
JLL describes coordinating hotel asset management and project delivery from concept, evaluating underused F&B and meeting space for alternative revenue uses.
- Source: JLL (2025), [Teaming up for value-up construction](https://www.jll.com/en-in/guides/teaming-up-for-value-up-construction).
- Transferable method: run room-night / ADR / occupancy and MICE/F&B models separately; flag double counting; test meeting capacity versus room supply and operator staffing.

**JLL-04 — Sustainable building case comparisons.**
JLL discusses Malaysia's Merdeka 118 and Tun Razak Exchange, relating sustainability credentials and occupier preferences.
- Source: JLL, [Rising tenant demand for green office buildings](https://www.jll.com/en-sea/insights/rising-tenant-demand-for-green-office-buildings-an-opportunity-for-commercial-assets-in-kuala-lumpur).
- Transferable method: evaluate certification costs and tenant demand only where substantiated; do not lift the case's rental/occupancy outcomes into a Thai forecast.

## Anthropic — robust AI agent engineering and operational value

**AN-01 — Anthropic's published multi-agent Research architecture.**
The firm describes a lead researcher, independently delegated parallel work, iterative searching, persistent artifacts, citation verification and evaluation; it also discusses coordination overhead and source-quality risks.
- Source: Anthropic (2025), [How we built our multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system).
- Transferable method: bounded agent assignments, separate evidence capture and synthesis, direct file outputs, critique/citation checks, run logs, fail-closed source handling, and reproducible evals.

**AN-02 — Building effective agents: composable workflows vs autonomy.**
Anthropic publishes patterns for prompt chaining, routing, parallelization, evaluator/optimizer loops, and appropriate complexity.
- Source: Anthropic (2024), [Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents).
- Transferable method: use deterministic arithmetic and explicit workflows for financial outputs; reserve autonomous tool use for open-ended research; require evals and human acceptance for high-stakes outputs.

**AN-03 — Public implementation ecosystem and customer examples.**
Anthropic's public customer library lists applications including property management communication, financial workflows, customer support, and observability.
- Source: Anthropic, [Customer stories](https://www.anthropic.com/customers).
- Transferable method: define concrete operational jobs, baseline cost/accuracy, escalation, privacy and robustness before claiming AI savings. Customer stories are not independent efficacy studies.

## Bain Capital — environmental technology as disciplined, measurable investment

**BC-01 — Bain Capital Double Impact published investment process.**
Bain Capital Double Impact describes sourcing, commercial/financial/impact diligence, active value creation and measurable impact management.
- Source: Bain Capital Double Impact, [Approach](https://www.baincapitaldoubleimpact.com/approach).
- Transferable method: pair environmental materiality with verified business case, accountable operating owner, measurable targets, portfolio governance, and ongoing monitoring.

**BC-02 — AqueoUS Vets and Japan Wind Development.**
The public Double Impact portfolio identifies water-contamination treatment and wind-development investments.
- Source: Bain Capital Double Impact, [Portfolio](https://www.baincapitaldoubleimpact.com/portfolio).
- Transferable method: evaluate water treatment and renewable technologies as distinct engineering propositions with throughput, local regulation, implementation and asset maintenance requirements. Portfolio inclusion is not proof of a technology's suitability for this project.

**BC-03 — Eleda infrastructure services and green-transition demand.**
Bain Capital describes a partnership involving Eleda, which operates in water, power distribution, district heating, transport and charging infrastructure.
- Source: Bain Capital, [Eleda investment announcement](https://www.baincapital.com/news/eleda-gets-new-ownership-altor-sells-controlling-stake-bain-capital-companies-form-partnership).
- Transferable method: consider installation and maintenance service capacity, long-term contracts, outsourcing, local labor and supplier concentration—not only initial green CAPEX.

## How to extend the corpus responsibly
1. Choose cases by **relevance to the proposed development** (not by employer prestige).
2. Prefer primary deal announcements, official published methods and detailed signed reports over secondary promotional summaries.
3. For each new case, record: institution, project/transaction, **actual role** (adviser, banker, investor, operator, researcher), year, geography, reported actions, reported results, publicly disclosed constraints, direct URL and access date.
4. Write a separate **our proposed adaptation** explaining why it might transfer and what would invalidate it.
5. Require independent corroboration for consequential external statistics where possible.
6. Stop searching when sources become repetitive; do not claim to have inspected every case at a firm. Never reuse proprietary content beyond short attributed references.
